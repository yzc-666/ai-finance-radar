Source tiers

Source tier describes how evidence may be used. It does not score whether an organization or product is commercially successful.

Tier 1

Primary and official

Regulatory filings and notices, company newsrooms, official product documentation, and first-party investor materials. These sources can support publication when all record checks pass.

Tier 2

Established reporting

Named, accountable newsrooms and specialist publications. These are useful for discovery and context, but a media-only claim is held for additional verification.

Tier 3

Lead sources

Aggregators, databases, newsletters, and social posts. They can identify a lead, but they do not qualify a record for the public event feed.

02

The publication gate

An event appears publicly only after the minimum evidence and integrity checks below succeed.

  1. 1
    Concrete, attributable claim

    The record describes an observable product, partnership, customer, funding, acquisition, or regulatory development—not a prediction or promotional theme.

  2. 2
    Resolvable primary evidence

    The primary URL works, identifies its publisher, and supports the central claim. Secondary reporting may add context but cannot replace missing evidence.

  3. 3
    Normalized identity and date

    Companies map to stable IDs; country, sector, event type, dates, and retrieval time follow the public record contract.

  4. 4
    Duplicate and integrity checks

    Stable IDs, slugs, source URLs, and content hashes prevent silent duplication or unreviewed change.

  5. 5
    Verified status

    Only records explicitly marked verified are rendered into detail pages and the RSS feed. Discovery candidates remain outside production.

04

Corrections

Include the affected record ID or URL, describe the issue precisely, and attach a primary source where possible. Accepted corrections update the underlying data rather than hiding prior Git history.
